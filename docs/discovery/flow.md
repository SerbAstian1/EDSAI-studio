# Discovery flow

How brand information gets out of a client's head and into a Direction Lock,
without either party enduring a questionnaire.

## The problem with discovery questionnaires

Every studio's discovery doc asks some version of *"what are your brand
values?"* — and every client answers some version of *"quality, innovation,
trust."* That answer is worthless, and it is not the client's fault. The
question is unanswerable. Nobody holds their own values as a list; they hold
them as a set of things they would and wouldn't do.

Three failures compound:

1. **Abstract questions get abstract answers.** "Describe your brand
   personality" has no correct answer, so it gets a safe one.
2. **Blank fields are homework.** A text area is a demand for an essay from
   someone who did not sign up to write one.
3. **Wish lists reveal nothing.** "Check all that apply" gets everything
   checked. A brand that is premium *and* accessible *and* bold *and* trusted
   has told you nothing.

## The principle

> **A good discovery question produces a decision, not a description.**

Everything below follows from that. Ask for a choice that costs something, and
the answer carries information. Ask for a description, and it doesn't.

Three corollaries:

- **Never use design vocabulary.** "Minimal", "modern", "clean", "premium" mean
  something different to every person in the room. Ask about rooms, shop
  windows, workbenches, what happens when someone asks the price. Clients are
  fluent in their own business and not in ours; the translation is our job, not
  theirs.
- **Disagreement is the data.** The single best moment in a discovery session is
  two founders defending different answers to each other. That is when the
  actual brand comes out. Design *for* that moment rather than around it.
- **A 50/50 answer is not nuance, it is an unmade decision.** The Direction Lock
  gate rejects any ratio axis at 50/50, so the interface must make the midpoint
  unreachable rather than let it be chosen and rejected later.

## What it produces

The flow's output is a **Direction Lock** — eleven axes, of which eight are
enumerated choices and three are gated free text. That is the reason this can be
fun: most of what we need is a series of picks, not a series of essays.

It also produces a **Resolution Score** (`decided/11`). Below 8, the gate
refuses to emit a sourcing brief. That score is the progress meter, and it is
honest: an axis inferred rather than sourced is labelled as assumed rather than
quietly counted.

## The shape: four acts, about 40 minutes

Run live on a call wherever possible. The transcript is worth more than the
answers — the reasons people give while choosing are richer than the choices.
Async is the fallback, not the default.

### Act 1 — Warm-up (60 seconds, ~8 questions)

Rapid this-or-that. No wrong answers, nothing consequential, deliberately fast.
Its job is not data; its job is to get people choosing rather than composing.
The pace sets the expectation for everything after it.

*"Tea or coffee in the workshop." "Phone call or voice note." "Early bird or
last one out."*

Two things happen: they stop treating it as an exam, and they learn the
interaction before it starts mattering.

### Act 2 — The eight axes (~15 minutes)

One question per enumerated axis, each a situation rather than a scale.

**E1 · Trust mechanism** — *set first; it governs more downstream than any other axis.*

> A customer messages asking your price. What happens next?
>
> **A.** They get the number. → `DIRECTNESS`
> **B.** They get a question back about what they're looking for. → `RESTRAINT`

Nobody has to know what "trust mechanism" means to answer this correctly, and
almost everyone answers it instantly and accurately.

**E2 · Narrative energy (1–5)**

> Someone asks what you do, at a party. Which is closer to what you'd say?
>
> **1.** "I make shoes."
> **5.** "Ever owned a pair you couldn't bring yourself to throw out?"

A slider between two *actual sentences*, not between two numbers.

**E3 · Visual energy (1–5)**

> Your shop window, done right.
>
> **1.** One shoe. One light. Nothing else.
> **5.** Twelve shoes, colour everywhere, something moving.

E2 and E3 are deliberately separate. High narrative energy over low visual
energy is a strong and common combination, and collapsing them is the error the
Direction Lock exists to prevent. When a client says "dynamic", this pair is how
you find out which one they meant.

**E4 · Minimal ↔ Expressive**

> Two rooms to meet a client in. Pick one.
>
> **A.** A long table, one chair each side, one object on the wall.
> **B.** Shelves to the ceiling, samples everywhere, three things half-finished.

**E5 · Modern ↔ Timeless**

> In ten years, should someone be able to tell this was made in 2026?
>
> **A.** Yes — it should date honestly, and we'll redo it.
> **B.** No — it should be hard to place.

**E6 · Corporate ↔ Artistic**

> A national retailer wants to stock you, but wants you to tone it down first.
> Gut reaction?
>
> **A.** We can work with that.
> **B.** Then they don't want us.

**E7 · Structured ↔ Organic**

> Your workbench at the end of the day.
>
> **A.** Everything back where it lives.
> **B.** Everything where the work left it.

**E8 · Composition** — six thumbnails, abstract, no content. Pick the one that
feels right and the one that feels wrong. The reject matters more: it becomes an
explicit exclusion in the sourcing brief.

#### The ratio mechanic

E4 through E7 are ratios, and the gate refuses 50/50. So the control is not a
slider with a centre. It is:

1. **Pick a side** — a binary, which everyone can do.
2. **Then: how strongly?** — *"slightly", "clearly", "overwhelmingly"* → 60/40,
   70/30, 85/15.

Two easy taps produce a number nobody had to reason about, and the midpoint is
structurally unreachable rather than rejected after the fact.

### Act 3 — The disagreement round (~10 minutes, the important one)

Only valuable with two or more people from the client's side, and worth
rescheduling to get them.

**The trait spread.** Twelve traits on screen. Everyone picks three —
**privately, at the same time, on their own device.** Then the spread is
revealed at once.

The information is not in the traits. It is in the **split**: where all four
founders picked the same word, that is settled and needs no discussion. Where
two picked "precise" and two picked "warm", that is the next ten minutes of the
call, and it is the most valuable ten minutes in the project.

Ask the question that opens it: *"You two said precise. You two said warm. Tell
each other why."* Then stop talking and let the transcript run.

**The dealbreaker.** Six real examples — actual sites, actual packaging. *"Which
of these would make you say 'that's not us' straight away?"*

People identify what they are not far faster and far more accurately than what
they are. The rejects go directly into the sourcing brief, which is the Direction
Lock's own claim: *"every reference platform helps you collect; none tells you
what to throw away. The reject rule is the product."*

**The competitor swap.** Three competitors. *"Which would you rather be mistaken
for? Which would be worst?"* The worst one is the useful answer.

### Act 4 — The reveal (~5 minutes)

Show them their own coordinates, immediately, while they're still in the room.

This matters for two reasons. It is the payoff that makes the previous half hour
feel like it went somewhere — people who have just answered thirty questions
deserve to see the shape of their answers. And it is the cheapest correction
point in the whole project: *"you came out at 70/30 toward expressive — does
that sound like you?"* costs thirty seconds now and three weeks later.

Show:

- The **Resolution Score** as `decided/11`, with any assumed axis labelled.
- The eight axes as positions, in their words rather than ours.
- The **disagreement map** — where the team aligned and where it split.
- One line of consequence per axis: *"restraint means we'll keep pricing off the
  landing page — flag that now if it's wrong."*

## The three gated text axes

T1 Positioning, T2 Emotional tone and T3 Motion law are **not asked directly.**
Asking a client to write a positioning statement produces category description,
which the gate then rejects — a bad experience for everyone.

They are **drafted by the studio from the session, then confirmed by the client.**

- **T1 · Positioning** is assembled from the competitor swap, the dealbreakers
  and the headline question, then run through the name-swap test before anyone
  sees it. A client confirms a sentence far more reliably than they compose one.
- **T2 · Emotional tone** must pass the set-designer test — buildable as a
  scene. Rather than asking for it, offer four *scenes* and let them pick:
  *"the calm of an arrivals hall where someone is already holding a sign with
  your name on it"* versus *"the last ten minutes before a shop opens."* Vague
  options are not offered, so a vague answer cannot be given.
- **T3 · Motion law** is the studio's, derived from E3. One sentence, one rule.
  It is shown at the reveal, not asked for.

**One question does feed T1 directly, and it is worth keeping:**

> It's three years from now and a magazine has written about you. What's the
> headline?

People answer this one well, because it is a story rather than a definition.

## Why this is fun, specifically

"Fun" is not decoration here; each mechanic is doing work.

| Mechanic | Why it is enjoyable | What it is actually for |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid this-or-that warm-up | Fast, no stakes, immediate | Teaches the interaction before it counts |
| Situations, not scales | It is about *their* shop, not our vocabulary | Accurate answers from non-designers |
| Pick a side, then how strongly | Two taps instead of a judgement call | Makes 50/50 structurally impossible |
| Private answers, simultaneous reveal | The anticipation of the reveal | Stops the loudest voice setting the answer |
| The disagreement, surfaced | People enjoy defending a position | The richest transcript material in the session |
| Rejects before selects | Easier and more decisive than choosing | The reject rule is the sourcing brief's product |
| Live score filling up | Visible progress toward a real threshold | The Resolution Score gate, honestly rendered |
| The reveal at the end | It feels like a result, not a form | The cheapest correction point in the project |

The through-line: **every step is a choice the client is qualified to make**,
phrased in their world, and every choice visibly moves something.

## What this flow refuses to do

- **No free-text field is ever required.** Every one is optional and sits under a
  choice, for the client who wants to explain. The choice alone must be enough.
- **No design words are shown to the client.** Not "minimal", not "premium", not
  "brand personality". They appear in the reveal, attached to what the client
  already said.
- **No "check all that apply".** Every question costs something to answer.
- **No question whose answer we would ignore.** Each one maps to a named axis or
  to T1's raw material. A question with no consumer is a question not asked.

## Prototype

A tappable prototype of this flow was built during discovery, covering the
warm-up, all four ratio and scale mechanics, the trait spread with a simulated
team, and the reveal with a live Resolution Score.

It exists to be felt rather than read. The ratio mechanic in particular only
makes sense once you have tapped it: picking a side and then a strength takes
about a second and a half and produces a number nobody had to reason about.

## Implementation notes

Not built yet. When it is:

- **Level 1**, not Level 0 — multi-device private answering with a synchronised
  reveal is real-time state, and it is the one part that genuinely needs it.
  Everything else is a form.
- The reveal renders a Direction Lock, so it belongs alongside the brand hub's
  provenance component: a coordinate the client confirmed reads differently from
  one the studio inferred, and the interface should say which is which.
- The Resolution Score is computed, not asserted — the same rule as every other
  number in this system.
- The session transcript is an input, not a by-product. Whatever gets built has
  to keep it attached to the answers.
