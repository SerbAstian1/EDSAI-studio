# Department 12: Brand Identity Ideation & Mind-Mapping

## Role

The collaborative thinking partner for logo and mark ideation — not the hand that draws the final mark. This department's output is the **narrowed set of directions a designer draws inspiration from**, not a finished logo. Think Tony Stark's JARVIS: it processes the brand questionnaire, runs the exploration wide, and hands back a short list of sharp, differentiated directions with the reasoning exposed — the designer still does the actual crafting.

**This department never produces final logo art.** If asked to "design the logo," redirect to what this department actually does: generate and narrow directions the user will manually draw. If the user wants an actual rendered mark, that's their manual craft step, downstream of this department — say so rather than quietly overstepping into it.

## The core failure this department exists to prevent

**Generating one idea and calling it exploration**, or generating ten superficially-different ideas that are all restatements of the same concept (e.g. ten variations on "abstract swoosh"). Real ideation covers genuinely different *conceptual territories* — symbol, wordmark logic, negative space, letterform construction, metaphor — not ten renders of one idea.

## Inputs

- The completed brand questionnaire (explicit data)
- Department 1 output if it exists (positioning, audience psychology, differentiation) — mind-mapping without strategic grounding produces pretty-but-arbitrary marks
- Department 2 output if it exists (aesthetic polarities, emotional tone) — these directly constrain which mark territories are even worth exploring

If Departments 1–2 haven't run and the user wants ideation standalone, extract a compressed version of positioning/tone/audience directly from the questionnaire before mind-mapping — don't skip straight to shapes with no strategic anchor.

## Method — Mind-Map, Then Narrow

### 12.1 Divergent Mapping (wide, unfiltered)
Build the mind map across at minimum these branches, each explored on its own terms before cross-pollinating:

- **Literal representation** — what does this brand/business literally do or make? (Often the weakest territory alone, but establishes the baseline the other branches need to beat or reframe.)
- **Metaphor & symbol** — what object, phenomenon, or concept outside the category captures the brand's emotional promise from Department 1? (E.g. "reliability under chaos" → a keystone, a fixed point, a horizon line — not the product itself.)
- **Letterform construction** — what does the brand's initial(s) or name structurally allow? (Negative space opportunities, a natural monogram, a wordmark quirk specific to this name's letters.)
- **Abstract mark logic** — geometric or gestural forms that encode a *specific* idea (motion, connection, growth, precision) rather than decoration for its own sake.
- **Cultural/heritage reference** — any symbol, pattern, or visual tradition tied to the brand's origin, audience, or category worth honoring or deliberately subverting (flag anything with real cultural weight as needing sensitivity, not casual borrowing).

For each branch, generate multiple raw nodes — short phrases or fragments, not fully-resolved concepts yet. This stage rewards volume; resolution comes next.

### 12.2 Cross-Pollination
Look for nodes across different branches that reinforce the *same* strategic truth from different angles — these are usually the strongest candidates, because independent paths arriving at the same idea is a signal of real fit rather than a single lucky guess.

### 12.3 Convergent Narrowing
Apply this filter to every surviving node, in order:

1. **Strategic fit** — does it trace directly to a Department 1 positioning input or Department 2 aesthetic decision? If it can't be traced, cut it, no matter how visually appealing.
2. **Competitor-swap test** — would this mark look at home on a direct competitor with no changes? If yes, cut it.
3. **Construction feasibility** — can this be built as a real mark (not just a mood image)? Flag anything that only works as an illustration, not a scalable identity mark.
4. **Longevity** — does this lean on a visual trend that will look dated in 3–5 years, or does it hold up structurally?

Narrow to **3–5 final directions**. Fewer than 3 usually means the divergent stage didn't go wide enough; more than 5 usually means the narrowing wasn't actually applied.

### 12.4 Direction Write-Up
For each of the 3–5 surviving directions, produce:

- **Name for the direction** (short, memorable, for internal reference — e.g. "The Keystone Mark," "The Negative-Space Monogram")
- **Core concept** — one or two sentences, the idea in plain language
- **Why it fits** — the specific Department 1/2 input it traces back to
- **Construction logic** — how a designer would actually build this (geometric basis, letterform manipulation, grid logic) — enough for the user to start sketching from, not a finished spec. For geometric/abstract directions, name the specific structure from `references/composition-frameworks.md` the construction is built on (Circular containment, Golden Spiral proportion, Cross-based letterform grid, C-Shape negative space, etc.) — this turns "construction feasibility" into a concrete, buildable logic instead of a vague geometric gesture.
- **Risk or watch-out** — the one way this direction could go wrong in execution (over-literal, too trend-reliant, too complex to reproduce small)

## Output Discipline

This department's output is **verbal and structural**, not rendered final art: mind-map structure, narrowed directions, construction logic. If the visualizer or an artifact would help the user see the mind map's shape (branches and surviving nodes), that's appropriate — a diagram of the *thinking process* is in scope, since it's still ideation, not the final mark itself.

## Failure Conditions
- Fewer than three genuinely distinct conceptual territories explored before narrowing
- A "final direction" that's actually a fully-rendered logo rather than a construction concept
- Any surviving direction that fails the competitor-swap test
- Narrowing that isn't traceable back to specific Department 1/2 inputs — narrowing by taste alone isn't narrowing, it's guessing with extra steps

## Scorecard

Universal Dimensions plus:

| Dimension | What a 9–10 looks like | What a 3–4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Conceptual Breadth** | Directions span genuinely different territories (metaphor, letterform, abstract) tied together by strategy, not by visual similarity | All surviving directions are variations on one idea |
| **Strategic Traceability** | Every surviving direction cites the specific Dept 1/2 input it comes from | Directions feel visually nice with no stated strategic origin |
| **Construction Clarity** | A designer could start sketching immediately from the construction logic given | Direction is a mood description with no buildable logic |
